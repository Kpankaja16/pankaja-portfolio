Problem

Consumer AI products lose trust at the edges — when a user says or does something the system didn't anticipate. My role is to define how the product should behave across intents, states, and edge cases before engineering builds it.

What I actually do

I lead product discovery and UX architecture for the product, translating user research, usability testing, and comparable analysis into MVP scope, wireframes, and prioritized decisions. I define AI interaction maps across user intents, conversation paths, product states, and response logic to keep the AI's behavior consistent and state-aware.

I build JSX-based interactive prototypes to validate interaction models before engineering investment, and convert testing insight directly into acceptance criteria and workflow improvements across onboarding, engagement, and real-time interaction.

Prototyping the medium, not just the flow

Because the product centers on an AI avatar/character, flat wireframes only tell part of the story. I'm building hands-on skill in Blender, Maya, and Character Creator 5 to prototype avatar behavior and presence directly — testing the experience in the medium users will actually feel it in, rather than approximating it in Figma.

MSIS Capstone · Team of 4 · May–Dec 2024

Cutting instructional review time 40% with a multimodal AI assistant.

Role: Product scoping & prioritization Stack: Python, SQL, RAG, LLaMA, BLIP2 Result: UOnline Capstone Showcase Finalist
ProblemManual accessibility review doesn't scale to 30K+ learners
UsersInstructional designers & course production teams
DecisionScope to visual layout, not just text
PrioritizationPython/SQL-driven recommendation logic
Outcome40% faster review, 30% higher compliance

Problem

Instructional designers reviewing courses for accessibility and content-alignment issues do it manually — slow, inconsistent, and hard to scale across a program supporting 30,000+ online learners.

Approach

Working with a four-person team, I translated stakeholder needs from instructional design staff into feature specs and acceptance criteria. The key scoping decision: accessibility issues often live in visual layout, not just written content — so the assistant needed to interpret Canvas course screenshots directly, not just parse text.

I used Python and SQL to support the recommendation logic that decided which flagged issues to prioritize, working directly with the technical build rather than handing off a spec and stepping away.

Solution

A multimodal assistant built on LLaMA and BLIP2 vision-language models with a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture — it reads a course's visual layout, flags accessibility issues, and recommends specific content-flow and design improvements.

Outcome

40%reduction in course content review time
30%improvement in accessibility compliance score
FinalistUOnline Capstone Showcase
Solo Product Project · Spring 2025

A lifestyle-based AI meal planner — from three personas to a live prototype.

Role: Solo PM Tools: Figma, Miro, JIRA, Canva Status: Live prototype deployed
ProblemGeneric meal apps ignore how lifestyle shapes need
DiscoveryPersona development, user-flow mapping in Miro
PrioritizationJIRA backlog & sprints
PrototypeFigma wireframes → live deployed build
ValidationPM review feedback, used as course case study

Problem

Generic meal-planning tools treat every user the same, but a manual laborer's nutritional needs and schedule look nothing like a desk worker's or a student's. I scoped a planner that starts from lifestyle, not just calories.

Users

Manual laborer — high-protein needs Working professional — weight loss Student — limited time to cook

Decisions & prioritization

I scoped a lifestyle-based meal plan generator using work type, goals, and dietary restrictions as inputs — rather than a generic calorie tracker. Backlog and sprints were managed in JIRA to narrow scope to what an MVP actually needed: plan generation, weekly customization, and snack recommendations, deferring real-time AI generation and grocery-API integration to a later phase.

Solution

A mobile-first experience prototyped in Figma — and unlike most academic PM projects, taken all the way to a working, deployed build you can use right now.

Outcome

A complete MVP concept with full design and logic flow, validated well enough that it was used as a discussion case study in the course itself — with positive feedback from classmates and mentors during formal PM review.

How I Work

Three things I believe, backed by the work above.

01

A flat wireframe can't tell you if an AI avatar feels right. I learn the tools the product actually uses — JSX, Figma, or now Blender and Maya — so I can test the real experience, not an approximation of it.

02

AI products break where users go off-script. I build interaction maps that plan for the unexpected path first, so the core flow is the easy part, not an afterthought.

03

A decision isn't done until it's measured.

Whether it's a 40% cut in review time or an 18% drop in event waste, I close the loop — testing insight becomes acceptance criteria, and acceptance criteria becomes a number I can point to.
